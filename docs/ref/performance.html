Performance#

This page shows where a frame's time goes and which settings change it. The numbers here come from benchmarks of Radix alone and of Radix against SparkJS and PlayCanvas on the same scenes, both measured on an Apple M3 Max in Chrome at 1920x1080. Treat them as relative: GPU times drift by up to 1.5x from run to run.

Where the time goes#

  • The draw takes 60-85% of the frame. Inside a scene its cost is fill: pixel ratio 2 (4x the pixels) made the ceramic cave's draw 2.6x slower. With many small splats the cost is per splat.
  • Projection is 2-5% of the frame. Packed splats (16 B) project about 30% faster than ext (32 B).
  • The GPU sort is cheap: at 10M splats it takes about 3, 4.7, and 7 ms at 16, 24, and 32 bits.
  • The WebGL2 path reads keys back and sorts them on a worker, outside the frame. That takes tens of milliseconds at millions of splats, but it is not part of the frame time. The order's upload and the keys pass do run inside frames, and they show up as stalls while it re-sorts.

Against SparkJS and PlayCanvas#

From the comparison of Oct 9, 2026: five scenes from 0.5M to 10M splats, timed one frame at a time (5 rounds) and in a vsync loop on a 120 Hz display (3 rounds). In the two interiors the camera moves while it turns, so distance sorts must re-sort. The bicycle is drawn with blurAmount: 0, preBlurAmount: 0.3, as it was trained. Results: the benchmarks page and the full report.

  • Same culling: Radix on WebGPU was 1.03x slower than PlayCanvas 2.23.1 on WebGPU (7% at most). By default PlayCanvas also culls splats under a 1-pixel radius and, on WebGPU, under 0.75 px² of opacity × area; at defaults it was 1.30x as fast. Those culls remove visible detail (most of the bicycle's wheel spokes on WebGPU), and its fixed 0.075 px² dilation, a quarter of the 0.3 px² of standard 3DGS training, draws splats smaller than trained, so its defaults do less work than a like-for-like render.
  • SparkJS 2.3.1 culls like Radix and has no WebGPU path. Radix on WebGPU was 1.37x as fast; at 10M splats (Grace Cathedral, inside), 9.2 ms against 16.5 ms.
  • WebGL2, where all three sort on a worker: at defaults Spark was 1.09x as fast as Radix and PlayCanvas 1.47x. Radix stalls in the frames that upload a new order, and a moving camera makes it re-sort all the time: on Grace its turning frames took 16 ms, against 10 ms turning in place. This is the main weakness of Radix's WebGL2 path.
  • Vsync: with culling matched, Radix on WebGPU held 120 fps on four scenes and 118 on Grace (108 at defaults); PlayCanvas on WebGPU held 120 on all. On Grace the WebGL2 worker sorts drop frames: Radix 81 fps and Spark 73, with 23% and 27% of frames late and p99 frames of 50 and 59 ms. Timed, Spark showed almost no stalls. PlayCanvas on WebGL2 held 116 fps.
  • Level of detail helps most with very large scenes: Spark can build a LoD tree on load and draw a budget of splats chosen for the view. Radix loads and saves LoD trees but does not yet choose LoD subsets itself; the Doom, Quake, SOG and LCC examples choose them in example code (Doom's and Quake's on a worker under a splat budget, examples/doom/lod/).
  • Loading a PLY to the first frame took Radix 0.3-2.2 s, Spark 0.1-1.6 s, and PlayCanvas 0.3-5.0 s.

Large scenes#

  • 39M splats (packed) loaded in 3.2 s and drew at 149 ms of GPU per frame on WebGPU; the per-splat draw cost is the limit.
  • 92M splats exceed the 256 MiB storage binding baseline, and the sorter throws an error that names the limit. With createRenderer({ limits: { maxStorageBufferBindingSize: "adapter" } }) they drew at 357 ms per frame on the M3. On WebGL2 the projection textures of 92M splats are not renderable, so its limit is somewhere between 39M and 92M splats.
  • Headset GPUs (Quest 3's Adreno 7xx) mostly offer 128 MiB storage bindings: 32M keys.

Measuring#

  • createRenderer({ profile: true }) (or ?profile=1) installs a GpuProfiler; set splatRenderer.phase = (name, run) => profiler.phase(name, run) for separate "project" and "sort" rows (The renderer).
  • splatRenderer.readSortedCount() reads the GPU sort's visible count; SplatProjector.programs counts compiled programs (it should stop growing after the first frames).
  • node scripts/bench.mjs --matrix <name> runs /build/test/bench/index.html in headless Chrome: files x modes x methods x bits, with GPU time per phase and screenshots (Checks).
  • Measure with the GPU otherwise idle: an emulator tab or another page rendering distorts the results.
